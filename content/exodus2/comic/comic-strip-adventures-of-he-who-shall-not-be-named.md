# Comic Strip Idea: The Adventures of He Who Shall Not Be Named

> **Status:** Full story arc drafted (opening → ending frame).

## Premise

A satirical comic strip about forum culture, moderation, and erasure — rhymes with the OCReMix / **Second Exodus** lane ([investigation](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/controlled_opposition/investigations/ocremix-polarization-privatization-controlled-opposition-investigation.md), [Second Exodus](./the-second-exodus-of-oc-remix.md)).

**Core gag — identity as deletion:** We never print a person's name. The protagonist (and anyone the forum has "handled") appears only as what the board leaves behind after an account wipe — placeholder copy in the style of:

- **"User has been removed"**
- **"[deleted]"**
- **"This account no longer exists"**
- Anonymous / guest-style attribution where a username used to be

The reader fills in the blank. The joke is that **moderation didn't just hide the post — it deleted the whole person from the record**, and the strip treats that ghost text as the character's actual name. **He Who Shall Not Be Named** is literally whoever the forum erased.

**Real-life seed (repo only — not in-strip text):** After a rival fork collapsed, temporary forums were the scene. The author was addressed as **he who shall not be named** when leadership **refused to state the username** and the community talked around the handle. The strip title is that insult turned serial. Cross-read: [OCR investigation §8.5](https://paradigmthreat.net/influence/controlled_opposition/investigations/ocremix-polarization-privatization-controlled-opposition-investigation.md).

## Naming / pronoun rules

Most narration and captions refer to the protagonist as **He** — capital **H**, ordinary third-person pronoun, not a printed handle.

| Rule | Why |
|------|-----|
| **He** only at the **start** of a sentence | Reads as normal English: *He walked away.* |
| **Never** capital **He** mid-sentence or at the end | *…and He left* or *…follow He* would echo **deity** capitalization (*He/Him*). We are **not** presenting a god — only a forum-erasure joke. |
| Mid/end of sentence → lowercase **he** | *The crowd watched as he shrank on the horizon.* |
| Forum UI / attribution → boilerplate | **"User has been removed"**, **[deleted]**, etc. — the board’s voice, not the pronoun rule. |
| Title / series name | **He Who Shall Not Be Named** stays the formal strip title; in-panel copy stays sparse. |

**Tone guardrail:** One capital letter at sentence-open is the whole gag. Push it further and the strip accidentally becomes scripture cosplay.

## Text vs image generation (important)

### In-strip text (dialog, captions, signs the reader reads as the story)

**Only two real-world names may appear in published strip copy:**

1. **OC ReMix** (or **OverClocked ReMix** — the compound / closed shop; basis of the comic)
2. **Second Exodus** (the idea / rally / mental break — not necessarily the repo essay title every time, but the phrase is allowed)

**Do not put in strip text:** usernames, other forums, game or album titles, publisher or company names, real staff handles, quoted official forum policy, or other scene-specific proper nouns. Use **generic stand-ins** in bubbles and captions (*the old album*, *the first walkout*, *a centralized monopoly model*, *a rival commons*, etc.). Readers who know the history will map them; everyone else still gets the joke.

**Fiction tropes OK in strip text:** Pharaoh, Industry, sword-in-the-stone, burning bush, exodus vocabulary — these are **metaphor**, not real-life scene references.

### Image-generation instructions (prompts, style briefs, thumbnails)

Prompts may describe **any visual register** — adventure-game sword pull, sacred burning bush, forum UI chrome, generic game icons, parody logos, crowd silhouettes, DAW screens, etc.

**Allowed in art / prompt direction:**

- Visual homages (heroic sword, bush on a mountain, empty utopia)
- **Invented** signage, fake forum chrome, generic icons, placeholder album art
- Incidental **parody** text in the background that is **not** canonical dialog (e.g. nonsense board names, blurred handles)

**Not allowed in art as readable “official” copy:**

- **Official text of the scene** — real OCR forum posts, real submission agreement wording, real usernames, real album or game titles as if licensed, real corporate logotypes with exact trademark strings, real mod quotes pulled verbatim from threads

**Rule of thumb:** Images can look like the internet; **readable text in the strip** stays generic unless it is **OC ReMix** or **Second Exodus**.

## Visual / dialog rules

- **Speech bubbles:** attributed to **"User has been removed"** (or variant), not a face with a real handle.
- **Avatar / profile slot:** empty silhouette, default guest icon, or broken `[avatar]` placeholder.
- **Crowd / mods:** may still have generic forum personas; the erased user is the one who reads as **system message cosplay**.
- **Tone:** dry, recognizable to anyone who has watched a thread vanish and a username turn into boilerplate — not a legal accusation against a specific host.

## Setting metaphor

**OverClocked ReMix** is drawn as a **real place** — a compound you can walk into, not a flat webpage. Think **theme-park / guild hall / closed shop** energy:

- Forum = **courtyard + gates + posted hours**
- Catalog / judge pipeline = **sword in the stone** at the center (adventure-game register: blade in anvil/stone, crowd gasps when it moves)
- Industry gravity = **the sword** everyone mistakes for the prize — access, legitimacy, “going pro,” **doing what you love** for money

The stone is **not** the community’s true gift; it is what **open hours** lend you and **closing time** takes back.

## Story / strips

### Arc 1 — Open hours (the borrowed sword)

1. **He enters the OC ReMix Forum** — drawn like stepping through gates into a physical compound, not logging into a tab.
2. **Center of the yard:** the **sword in the stone** (adventure-game read: heroic, mythic, obviously *not* ours to keep).
3. **He pulls it / wields it** — crowd reacts; he’s impressive *while the place is open*. The sword stands in for **being inside the system**: judged releases, visibility, the thrill of “this is the real scene.”
4. **Open hours end.** Alarm, staff energy, or posted schedule — **time’s up**.
5. **The sword goes back in the stone.** Ritual, not cruelty: same slot, same lock. He doesn’t get to walk home with it.
6. **Gates lock.** Lights shut off. **Everybody leaves.** The compound empties.
7. **He alone** stares at the **closed shop** of OC ReMix — dark windows, **CLOSED** sign, silent courtyard. No sword, no crowd, no borrowed legitimacy. Off-panel or small bubble: **“Damn it!”** *(first occurrence)*

**Beat:** Disillusionment. The magic was **rental**. The scene looked like destiny during business hours and like a parking lot after.

**Recurring motif — “Damn it!”:** The protagonist gets **blocked at every turn** — shop closes, exodus fails, utopia empty, forced to take a strategy he hates. Same exasperated line each time; reader learns the pattern before the punchline.

### Arc 2 — After a walk (the road not taken)

8. **He walks away** — small figure, closed compound behind him. No deity framing; just tired.
9. **Time passes** (montage strip — optional).
10. **He faces a crowd again** — outside the locked gates; generic **rival commons** / fork energy (visual TBD).
11. **The pitch — and for a moment, he has them.** He tells them the **sword in the stone** is **not** what Industry is offering. The real offer is **something else entirely**:

    - A **community that supports itself**
    - **Free products** made **for the fun of it**
    - **Distribution for entertainment** — not as a stealth competitor brand
    - Something that **does not mean to compete** with Industry
    - The **road not taken** for almost every fan community that **bows to industry influence** — especially the fantasy of making **a lot of money “doing what you love”** (making games, making remixes, making *content*)

**Thesis in one line for captions:** Industry sells the **sword**; the commons sells **each other**.

### Arc 3 — Second Exodus rant (crowd drains)

12. **He makes the case that a Second Exodus is necessary.** Communities will **not** be released from the grip of **Pharaoh** — i.e. **Industry** — that easily. The **first exodus** did little more than **divide the community further** (fork / walkout — generic; no other forum names in strip text).

13. **Second Exodus is not just leaving** the systems of control. It is leaving the **entire pattern of thought** behind — default **for-profit** framing, enclosure reflex, “respectability” as temperature control. *(Repo cross-read: [Second Exodus essay](./the-second-exodus-of-oc-remix.md) — not quoted verbatim in panels.)*

14. **Not mere selflessness — a different profit model.** The future of profit-making **won’t stay centralized** like the **old monopoly stack**; we should all realize that by now. The **real for-profit future** is **community-based**: a **support structure** that pays **members and contributors directly**. *That* is how someone might get **rich** in the future — **not** by **stealing and consolidating intellectual property**.

15. **During the rant:** panels show the crowd **thinning**. Most have **already left**, bored or hostile. **Those remaining** look **confused** — torn between his vision and **their quest to leave the communities they love**.

### Arc 4 — “Alright, let’s go” (Exodus 2 flop)

16. **He rallies the stragglers:** **“Alright, let’s go!”** — marching toward the horizon, **announcing Second Exodus slogans**, **anticipating success** (Exodus 2 flop energy: the first walkout had a caravan; this one doesn’t).

17. **Wide panel:** he’s small on the road. **Not a single person followed.**

18. Off-screen bubble: **“Damn it!”** *(second occurrence)*

### Arc 5 — Mountain, bush, utopia (build-it fail)

19. **Alone again**, he **wanders up a mountain**.

20. **Summit:** a **burning bush** — sacred-comedy register (exodus vocabulary, not reverent). **Burning bush:** *“If you build it, they will come.”* *(Pop trope — OK in strip; not a scene proper noun.)*

21. **He** (protagonist, deadpan): **“Yeah, right.”**

22. **Next frame / time skip:** He has **built a utopian paradise** — **open source** and **community integration at all levels**. Gorgeous. Empty.

23. **Grand opening:** he **announces** it’s open. **Cut to crowd elsewhere** — unrelated forum-goers chatting:

    - **“Did you hear something just now?”**
    - **“Absolutely not. I absolutely didn’t hear anything just now.”**

24. **Time passes.** He’s **disillusioned again**, **dusting off** the utopia — **untouched**, no footprints, no wear.

25. **Back up the mountain.** **He:** **“Okay, now what?”**

26. **Burning bush** (exasperated oracle energy): **“Announce a twenty-five-year anniversary for your old album project and use it to grab attention for what you built. Other than that, I have no freaking ideas, man.”** *(Generic in strip — no game or album title.)*

27. **He hates this strategy.** Face says: *this is the sword again* — attention hack, anniversary bait, industry-shaped marketing dressed as exodus.

### Arc 6 — End frame

28. **Final panel:** He sits **angrily, frustratingly composing music** — generic DAW / headphones / waveform energy (visual TBD). Muttered bubble: **“This had better work.”**

**THE END.**

## Production notes

- **In-strip names:** only **OC ReMix** and **Second Exodus** — see **Text vs image generation** above.
- **“User has been removed”** boilerplate gags can pepper crowd panels — generic forum voice, not a real quote.
- **Dialog:** rant beats can be captioned narration vs speech bubbles — pick per strip density when drawing.
- **Image prompts:** may specify sword-pull art, burning bush, fake forum UI, generic sci-fi album cover silhouette, etc.; do not render **official scene text** (real handles, real policy PDF strings, real game titles in legible type).
- **Anniversary beat:** in strip, **old album project** only; author maps meaning privately.
